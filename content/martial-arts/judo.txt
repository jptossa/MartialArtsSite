JUDO
"The gentle way, with a lot of throwing."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Japan
  Category:    Grappling
  Difficulty:  Intermediate
  Focus:       Throws, takedowns, pins, falling safely

WHAT IT IS
Judo ("the gentle way") is a Japanese grappling art centered on throwing an
opponent to the ground using balance, timing, and leverage, then controlling
them with pins, chokes, or armlocks. Its guiding ideas are "maximum efficiency"
and "mutual welfare and benefit". You learn to use an opponent's force against
them and to practice safely so partners can train for many years.

HISTORY
Jigoro Kano founded judo in Tokyo in 1882, drawing on older jujutsu schools
and removing their most dangerous techniques so it could be practiced at full
effort. He built the Kodokan, which remains the art's headquarters. Judo became
an Olympic sport in 1964 (for women in 1992) and is one of the most widely
practiced martial arts in the world. It influenced Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and
modern wrestling-style grappling.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Ukemi: learning to fall safely, which comes first and is practiced every
    class.
  - Technique drills (uchikomi) where you repeat a throw's entry many times.
  - Throwing practice (nagekomi) with a cooperative partner.
  - Randori: live sparring where both people try to throw and pin.
  - Training is done in a judogi (a heavy jacket and pants) with a belt
    ranking system that starts at white and goes through colored and black belts.

CORE SKILLS
  Throws       Hip throws, shoulder throws, foot sweeps, and sacrifice throws.
  Takedowns    Off-balancing and bringing an opponent down under control.
  Pins         Holding someone on their back (osaekomi).
  Falling      Absorbing impact without injury.
  Newaza       Chokes and arm locks on the ground.

STRENGTHS
  - Teaches safe falling, a useful skill for everyday life.
  - Builds balance, grip strength, and functional strength.
  - Effective against larger opponents when timing is right.
  - Live sparring is common, so skills are tested against resistance.

LIMITATIONS
  - Relies on gripping clothing, which is less reliable against someone in
    thin or loose clothing, though many throws adapt.
  - No striking and little weapons content.
  - Hard throws can strain the shoulders, knees, and back.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Strong for dealing with a single attacker who closes in or grabs you. A solid
throw ends many fights, and good falling protects you if you are shoved or
slip. Combine it with striking and awareness for a complete approach.

GOOD FIT FOR
Active people who like physical, partner-based training, and parents looking
for a structured activity for children.
