SAMBO
"Judo and wrestling went east and came back armed with leg locks."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Soviet Union (Russia)
  Category:    Hybrid (throws, ground fighting, and optional striking)
  Difficulty:  Intermediate
  Focus:       Throws, leg locks, ground control, combat striking

WHAT IT IS
Sambo is a Russian martial art and sport built from the best of several
grappling traditions. The name is a Russian acronym meaning roughly
"self-defense without weapons". It has two main forms: sport sambo, a
grappling sport using a jacket, and combat sambo, which adds striking and is
closer to a self-defense and mixed martial arts approach.

HISTORY
Sambo was developed in the Soviet Union in the 1920s and 1930s for use by the
military and police. Its founders drew on judo, wrestling, and folk grappling
styles from across the Soviet republics, aiming for a fast, practical system.
It spread internationally during the 20th century and has produced many
well-known mixed martial arts competitors.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Warm-up and conditioning, with wrestling-style drills.
  - Throws and takedowns practiced against a partner in a short, belted jacket
    and shorts (sport sambo).
  - Ground work: pins, escapes, and leg locks. Sport sambo allows leg locks but
    not chokes.
  - Live sparring (rolling) in short, intense rounds.
  - Combat sambo classes add striking and protective gear.

CORE SKILLS
  Throws        Judo-style and wrestling-style throws and takedowns.
  Leg locks     Ankle locks, knee bars, and other leg attacks.
  Ground work   Pins, control, and escapes.
  Striking      Punches, kicks, and elbows, in combat sambo.
  Conditioning  Stamina, strength, and toughness built through live work.

STRENGTHS
  - A well-rounded mix of throws, ground control, and leg locks.
  - Techniques are tested in live, resisting practice.
  - Strong conditioning and fighting spirit.
  - Efficient and practical, with little wasted movement.

LIMITATIONS
  - Fewer schools than other grappling arts, particularly outside Russia and
    Eastern Europe, so finding a good club can take effort.
  - Leg locks can injure knees and ankles when applied or defended carelessly.
  - Sport sambo has no chokes, and striking is mostly limited to combat sambo.
  - Hard throws and pressure-heavy sparring put strain on the body.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Strong for taking control of a single opponent, whether by throwing them or
following them to the ground, with practical leg locks as a finish. Combat
sambo adds striking for a more complete approach.

GOOD FIT FOR
Athletic people who enjoy grappling, those interested in mixed martial arts,
and anyone who wants a practical blend of judo and wrestling with extra
leg attacks.
