MUAY THAI
"Why use two limbs when you have eight?"

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Thailand
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Intermediate
  Focus:       Kicks, elbows and knees, clinch, conditioning

WHAT IT IS
Muay Thai, "the art of eight limbs," is a striking art that uses fists, elbows,
knees, and shins, eight points of contact in all. It also includes a strong
clinch game, where fighters grapple in close to knee and throw each other.
Hard conditioning and powerful, committed strikes are its hallmarks.

HISTORY
Muay Thai developed from older Siamese battlefield combat arts and became a
popular spectator sport and national pastime in Thailand over several
centuries. It has deep cultural roots: fighters traditionally perform a
pre-fight dance (wai khru) to honor teachers and family. Thai boxing camps,
stadiums such as Lumpinee and Rajadamnern, and international gyms have since
spread the sport worldwide, and it is a staple of modern mixed martial arts.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Skipping rope, shadowboxing, and calisthenics to warm up.
  - Pad work with a trainer, who holds Thai pads for you to strike while
    correcting your form.
  - Heavy bag rounds, clinch drills, and technique practice.
  - Light or controlled sparring, building up to harder sparring if you choose.
  - Sessions are demanding. Expect a lot of rounds of three minutes.

CORE SKILLS
  Kicks             Round kicks to the legs, body, and head, thrown with the
                    shin and hip rotation. The teep (push kick) controls
                    distance.
  Elbows and knees  Short, damaging strikes for close range.
  Clinch            Controlling an opponent's head and posture to knee them,
                    off-balance them, or throw them.
  Conditioning      Cardio, core strength, and shin and leg toughness.

STRENGTHS
  - Excellent cardio and full-body strength.
  - Powerful strikes from every range.
  - Builds toughness and confidence under pressure.
  - Proven in sport competition with full contact.

LIMITATIONS
  - Heavy contact brings bruises and shin soreness. Injuries to knees,
    shins, and ankles are common in hard training.
  - Ground fighting is not part of the core art.
  - Intermediate learning curve: timing and balance take time to build.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
High for standing situations. Strong strikes, a sturdy clinch, and the ability
to take a hit are real assets. As with any sport art, remember that the
priority outside the gym is to escape, and that striking a person can have
legal consequences.

GOOD FIT FOR
People who want a hard workout, enjoy partner pad work, and are comfortable
with contact. Less suitable if you want a gentle, low-impact start.
