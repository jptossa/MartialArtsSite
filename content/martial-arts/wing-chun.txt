WING CHUN
"Economy of motion, no wasted effort."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      China
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Intermediate
  Focus:       Close-range striking, trapping, sensitivity drills, centerline

WHAT IT IS
Wing Chun is a southern Chinese kung fu style designed for close-quarters
combat. It teaches you to guard the centerline (the vertical line down the
middle of your body), to attack and defend in the same motion, and to use
straight, direct strikes with minimal wasted movement. It favors efficiency and
structure over power and athletic ability.

HISTORY
Legend credits a Buddhist nun, Ng Mui, and her student Yim Wing Chun with
creating the style, but the story is largely oral tradition and can't be
verified. What is documented is that the style spread through the Foshan
region of Guangdong in the 19th century, then through Hong Kong in the
20th. Grandmaster Yip Man taught it there, and his student Bruce Lee brought it
to global attention. Today it exists in many lineages that differ in detail.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Forms, solo sequences that teach structure: Siu Nim Tao, Chum Kiu, and
    Biu Jee.
  - Chi sao ("sticky hands"): partners keep contact with each other's forearms
    and train to feel and react to pressure.
  - Drills on a wooden dummy (mook jong) to train structure and angles.
  - Pad work and partner drills for straight punching and quick chain punches.
  - Pace is usually low impact. Full-contact sparring is rare in traditional
    schools, though some do it.

CORE SKILLS
  Centerline       Protecting and attacking along the body's midline.
  Close striking   Short, rapid chain punches and palm strikes.
  Trapping         Pinning or controlling an opponent's limbs at close range.
  Sensitivity      Reacting to touch instead of sight.
  Structure        Stable stance and elbow position that make a strike strong.

STRENGTHS
  - Efficient, compact movement that works in tight spaces.
  - Builds reflexes and coordination.
  - Low-impact training, suitable for a wide range of ages and fitness levels.

LIMITATIONS
  - Many schools do little live sparring, so skills can go untested against
    a resisting, unpredictable opponent. Ask before joining.
  - Limited kicking, takedown, and ground content.
  - Results vary widely between lineages and teachers.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Good ideas about structure, close distance, and fast, direct counters. The
value depends greatly on whether the school trains against resistance.
Look for one that includes pressure-testing in addition to forms.

GOOD FIT FOR
People who enjoy technical detail and feeling, and those who want a
lower-impact art. Less suited to anyone who wants hard sparring right away.
