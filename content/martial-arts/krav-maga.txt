KRAV MAGA
"No belts, no ceremony, no nonsense."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Israel
  Category:    Hybrid (striking, grappling, threat response)
  Difficulty:  Beginner
  Focus:       Threat response, striking, weapon defense, stress inoculation

WHAT IT IS
Krav Maga ("contact combat" in Hebrew) is a self-defense system built for
real-world violence. It borrows from boxing, wrestling, judo, and other arts
and keeps only what works quickly under stress. The aim is to survive and
escape: neutralize the threat, then get to safety. It is a system for
practical use, not a sport.

HISTORY
Imi Lichtenfeld, a Hungarian-born athlete trained in boxing and wrestling,
began developing the system in Czechoslovakia in the 1930s to defend his
community against street violence. After he settled in what became Israel, he
taught it to the Israeli military and security forces, and it was refined for
soldiers. A civilian version spread worldwide from the 1970s onward. Because
there is no single governing body, schools and associations vary in quality
and in curriculum.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Conditioning and a brief technique warm-up.
  - Drilled responses to specific attacks: chokes, grabs, headlocks, punches,
    and knife or gun threats, taught with a partner and protective gear.
  - Scenario and stress drills: multiple attackers, fatigue, noise, surprise,
    and attacks from awkward positions such as sitting or lying down.
  - Most schools use level or patch systems for progress. Many don't use belts
    at all.

CORE SKILLS
  Threat response   Reading a situation, using distance, and deciding whether
                    to leave, de-escalate, or act.
  Striking          Simple, forceful punches, palm strikes, elbows, knees, and
                    kicks to vulnerable targets.
  Weapon defense    Controlling or avoiding armed attackers, with the strong
                    advice that running is usually better.
  Stress training   Learning to act while adrenaline is high and breathing
                    is hard.

STRENGTHS
  - Basics are quick to pick up. Beginners can learn useful responses in weeks.
  - Scenario-based training reflects how real attacks begin.
  - Builds awareness, decision-making, and confidence.
  - Good conditioning without requiring prior fitness.

LIMITATIONS
  - Techniques are simplified and not usually tested in fully resisting
    sparring, so how well they work depends heavily on the school.
  - Quality varies a lot with no common standard.
  - Some drills involve high-risk actions that can only be practiced in a
    controlled, partial-contact way.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Directly focused on it. The strongest schools stress awareness, avoidance, and
escape, backed by realistic practice. Ask any school about live sparring,
safety, and instructor experience before joining.

GOOD FIT FOR
Complete beginners wanting practical skills fast, people focused on
personal safety rather than competition, and those who like scenario-based
training.
