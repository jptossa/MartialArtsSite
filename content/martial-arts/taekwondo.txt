TAEKWONDO
"Fast feet, sharp technique, and a very good stretch."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      South Korea
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Beginner
  Focus:       Kicking, forms (poomsae), sparring, flexibility

WHAT IT IS
Taekwondo ("the way of the foot and fist") is a Korean striking art best known
for its fast, high, and spinning kicks. Training combines kicking technique,
patterned forms called poomsae, step-by-step self-defense routines, and
sparring. It places a strong emphasis on discipline, courtesy, and perseverance.

HISTORY
Taekwondo took shape in Korea in the 1940s and 1950s, when several martial arts
schools combined Korean traditions with karate and other influences. The name
"taekwondo" was adopted in 1955. Two main organizations later developed: the
International Taekwondo Federation (ITF) and World Taekwondo (WT). WT
sparring is the style seen in the Olympic Games, where taekwondo has been a
medal sport since 2000.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Stretching and conditioning, with a lot of work on hip and leg flexibility.
  - Kicking drills on pads and shields, often in lines or in pairs.
  - Poomsae: forms performed alone that link stances, blocks, and strikes.
  - Sparring in protective gear, with points scored for clean, controlled
    strikes.
  - Students wear a dobok (uniform) and advance through colored belts to black
    belt, with graded tests.

CORE SKILLS
  Kicks         Front, roundhouse, side, back, and spinning kicks, with an
                emphasis on speed, height, and accuracy.
  Poomsae       Forms that build balance, stance, and technique.
  Sparring      Timing, distance, and reacting to a live partner.
  Flexibility   Hip and leg mobility that supports high kicks.
  Footwork      Quick stepping, angling, and closing the gap.

STRENGTHS
  - Clear, structured progression with visible goals and belt tests.
  - Builds flexibility, balance, and leg strength.
  - Good for all ages, and widely taught to children.
  - Fast, long-range kicking that keeps an opponent at a distance.

LIMITATIONS
  - Sport sparring rules focus on kicks and limit punching and grappling, so
    the competition style can leave gaps in close-range and ground skills.
  - Many schools do little hard or pressure-tested sparring.
  - High kicks are less practical in everyday clothing and tight spaces.
  - Style and quality vary between schools and between the ITF and WT.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Good for building distance, timing, and strong low kicks, and for general
fitness and confidence. Use it with an awareness of its limits: it teaches
very little about grabs, grappling, or fighting at close range. Look for a
school that includes self-defense and realistic sparring.

GOOD FIT FOR
Children and teens, people who enjoy structured progression and athletic
kicking, and anyone who wants flexibility and fitness along with a martial art.
