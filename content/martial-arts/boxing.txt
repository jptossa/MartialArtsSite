BOXING
"Two fists, one very good idea."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      United Kingdom (modern form)
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Beginner
  Focus:       Punching, footwork, head movement, timing

WHAT IT IS
Boxing is a striking art that uses only punches. It builds sharp technique in
four areas: punching, defense, footwork, and timing. The skill ceiling is high
even though the rules are simple, and that is much of the appeal. Throwing a
clean combination while tired and being hit back is harder than it looks.

HISTORY
Fist fighting is ancient, with contests recorded in Greece and Rome. The modern
sport took shape in Britain in the 18th and 19th centuries, with formal rules
such as the London Prize Ring rules and then the Marquess of Queensberry
rules (1860s), which introduced gloves, rounds, and the ten-second count. It
has been an Olympic sport since 1904 and has produced some of history's most
famous athletes.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Skipping rope, shadowboxing, and mobility work.
  - Heavy bag and speed bag, plus focus mitts with a coach.
  - Partner drills for defense and combinations.
  - Optional sparring, usually light and gradual with protective gear.
  - Fitness is a major component: roadwork and bodyweight conditioning.
  You can start at a gym with no contact at all, as a fitness class.

CORE SKILLS
  Punching       Jab, cross, hook, and uppercut, and chaining them together.
  Footwork       Moving in, out, and around an opponent while staying balanced.
  Head movement  Slipping, bobbing, and weaving to avoid being hit.
  Timing         Knowing when to throw, counter, or step away.

STRENGTHS
  - Easy to start, with a short list of basic techniques.
  - Excellent conditioning, coordination, and speed.
  - Sharpens reflexes and builds composure under pressure.
  - Requires little equipment and is available at many gyms.

LIMITATIONS
  - Only punches. No kicks, grappling, or ground defense.
  - Repeated head contact in hard sparring carries real long-term risk, so
    limit hard sparring and use proper protection.
  - Hand and wrist injuries without good wrapping and technique.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Solid for standing encounters. Good footwork, distance management, and the
ability to avoid and land punches transfer well. It does not cover grabs,
tackles, or weapons, so it works best alongside other training.

GOOD FIT FOR
Beginners, people seeking fitness and stress relief, and anyone wanting a
focused and efficient skill set.
