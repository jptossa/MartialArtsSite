KICKBOXING
"Boxing hands, plus the legs that boxing leaves at home."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Japan and the United States
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Beginner
  Focus:       Punch and kick combinations, footwork, conditioning, sparring

WHAT IT IS
Kickboxing is a stand-up striking sport that combines the punches and footwork
of boxing with kicks borrowed from karate and Muay Thai. Fights and sparring
are built around fast combinations of punches and kicks thrown from a mobile
stance. It is popular both as a competitive sport and as a high-energy fitness
class.

HISTORY
Kickboxing emerged in the 1950s and 1960s in Japan, where promoters and
fighters blended Muay Thai with karate, and in the United States in the 1970s,
where full-contact karate competitions led to rules that added kicks to boxing.
Rulesets vary by organization. Some allow only kicks above the waist, while
others add low kicks or knees. It has fed into mixed martial arts and
produced many well-known striking champions.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - A warm-up with skipping and shadowboxing.
  - Drilling combinations of jabs, crosses, hooks, and kicks on pads and heavy
    bags.
  - Footwork and head movement drills.
  - Light to moderate sparring with gloves, shin guards, and a mouthguard.
  - Conditioning circuits for cardio and core strength.

CORE SKILLS
  Combinations    Chaining punches and kicks into smooth sequences.
  Kicks           Round kicks, front kicks, and teeps to control distance.
  Footwork        Moving in and out of range and changing angles.
  Defense         Blocking, parrying, slipping, and checking kicks.
  Conditioning    Cardio, core strength, and stamina.

STRENGTHS
  - Easy to start, with a short list of core techniques.
  - Excellent cardio and full-body conditioning.
  - Builds timing, speed, and confidence under pressure.
  - Widely offered, from competitive gyms to fitness classes.

LIMITATIONS
  - Mostly standing striking: little or no grappling, clinch, or ground
    defense, depending on the ruleset.
  - Fitness-style classes often skip sparring and technique detail.
  - Hard sparring carries a risk of bruising, shin injuries, and head impacts,
    so limit hard contact and use proper protection.
  - Rulesets differ between schools, so what you learn can vary.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Good for striking from a distance, defending against punches and kicks, and
staying composed when hit. Pair it with awareness of its limits around
grabbing and ground fighting.

GOOD FIT FOR
Beginners who want striking, strong cardio, and a high-energy workout, and
people who want to try competitive stand-up fighting.
