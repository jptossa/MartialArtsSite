KARATE
"Fundamentals first. Yes, you'll practice the stance."

SNAPSHOT
  Origin:      Japan (developed in Okinawa)
  Category:    Striking
  Difficulty:  Beginner
  Focus:       Kata, blocking, kicking, sparring

WHAT IT IS
Karate ("empty hand") is a striking art that teaches punches, kicks, knee and
elbow strikes, and blocks. Training is built on three parts: kihon (basics),
kata (set forms), and kumite (sparring). It emphasizes discipline, respect, and
steady progress, with a belt system that makes advancement visible.

HISTORY
Karate developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom (now Okinawa), where local fighting
methods (te) combined with Chinese martial arts influence. It spread to the
Japanese mainland in the early 20th century, led by Gichin Funakoshi, who
founded Shotokan. Many styles followed, including Shotokan, Goju-ryu, Shito-ryu,
and Wado-ryu, plus the full-contact Kyokushin. Karate became popular
worldwide after the Second World War and was included in the Tokyo 2020 Olympics.

WHAT TRAINING LOOKS LIKE
  - Bowing in, warm-up, and stretching.
  - Kihon: repeated practice of stances, punches, kicks, and blocks.
  - Kata: choreographed sequences practiced alone and then analyzed with
    partners (bunkai) to understand the applications.
  - Kumite: sparring, ranging from point-based and controlled to full-contact
    depending on the style.
  - Colored belts lead to black belt. Testing at each level is normal.

CORE SKILLS
  Kata        Forms that build memory, balance, and technique.
  Blocking    Deflecting and intercepting attacks.
  Kicking     Front, roundhouse, side, and back kicks with good control.
  Sparring    Timing, distance, and reacting to a live partner.

STRENGTHS
  - Clear, structured progression with visible goals.
  - Builds discipline, focus, and respect.
  - Good for all ages, and one of the most common arts for children.
  - Strong foundation in stance, posture, and basic striking.

LIMITATIONS
  - Styles and schools vary greatly. Some do little realistic sparring.
  - Limited grappling and ground fighting.
  - Point-sparring habits can differ from real fighting situations.
  - Progress through kata alone may not prepare you for resistance.

SELF-DEFENSE VALUE
Moderate to good, depending on the school. Contact-heavy styles with regular
sparring do the best job of preparing you. The discipline and fundamentals
are valuable. For practical protection, choose a school that tests technique
against resistance.

GOOD FIT FOR
Beginners, families, children and teens, and anyone who likes clear structure
and long-term goals.
